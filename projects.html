Large-scale parallelization of relativistic DFT

My main thesis project, and the subject of my first-author paper.

Quasi-four-component relativistic DFT, made parallel

MPI · Fortran · HPC

Heavy elements need relativistic quantum mechanics — their inner electrons move fast enough that ignoring relativity gives the wrong answer. The problem is that relativistic methods are expensive, and the standard implementations don't spread across a supercomputer well.

I designed and implemented MPI-based parallel and memory-distributed algorithms for quasi-four-component relativistic DFT with numeric atom-centered orbitals.

  • Achieved strong scaling across 1,000+ CPU cores and multiple nodes.
  • Distributing memory removed the per-node ceiling that previously blocked heavy-element systems entirely.
  • Enabled accurate simulation of heavy-element molecules and solids that were out of reach before.
  • Shipped into FHI-aims, so other research groups get it for free.

Machine-learned force fields

Replacing expensive quantum calculations with models that are nearly as accurate and far faster.

MACE potentials for relativistic DFT

MACE · PyTorch

A quantum calculation of the forces between atoms is accurate but slow — too slow to simulate anything for very long. A machine-learned interatomic potential learns that same energy surface from data, then evaluates it orders of magnitude faster.

I trained and validated MACE-based potentials that reproduce relativistic potential-energy surfaces at a fraction of the underlying cost.

  • Built the data pipeline turning large computational datasets into training sets.
  • Designed validation protocols to catch silent accuracy regressions — the failure mode that matters most when a model replaces ground truth.
  • Tuned architecture and training against a hard accuracy-versus-cost tradeoff.
  • Unlocked molecular-dynamics sampling at timescales direct DFT cannot reach.
